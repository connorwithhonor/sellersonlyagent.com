How Home Buying Actually Works in Santa Clarita

By Connor MacIvor, sellers-only agent · SYNC Brokerage · CalDRE #01238257

Most "how to buy a home" guides are written by agents who want to be your agent. Mine isn't. I represent sellers — which means I have exactly zero reason to sugarcoat any of this for you. I just want Santa Clarita buyers walking into my sellers' open houses prepared and confident. So here's the real map, in order.

Before anything else: buying a home in Santa Clarita is roughly a 30–45 day process once you're in contract — but the part most buyers skip (getting truly ready) is what decides whether you win the home you want. Don't start at the open house. Start here.

The eight steps, honestly

1

Get your money straight

Down payment, closing costs (budget ~2–3% of price), and reserves. Know your real out-the-door number before you fall in love with a house.

2

Get pre-approved — not pre-qualified

A real pre-approval (pulled credit, verified income) is what makes a Santa Clarita seller take your offer seriously. Pre-qualified is a guess; pre-approved is a green light.

3

Get a buyer's agent who represents you

Yes — I'll say it plainly: you want your own agent on the buy side. I'll even point you to good ones. (Post-NAR, you'll sign a buyer-broker agreement and discuss how that agent is paid. Know those terms going in.)

4

Tour with intent — open houses are your friend

This is where Santa Clarita Open Houses earns its name. Walk homes, learn the neighborhoods (Valencia, Saugus, Newhall, Canyon Country, Stevenson Ranch, Castaic), and calibrate what your budget really buys.

5

Write a smart offer

Price is only part of it. Terms, contingencies, close timeline, and concessions often win over a slightly higher number. This is where good representation pays for itself.

6

Open escrow & do your due diligence

Inspections, disclosures, appraisal, loan. You have contingency windows to investigate and to walk if something's wrong. Use them — don't waive protection just to "win."

7

Remove contingencies & lock the loan

Once you're satisfied and the lender's clear, you remove contingencies. This is the point of real commitment — go in eyes open.

8

Sign, fund, record — keys

Final signing, the loan funds, the deed records with the county, and the home is yours. In Santa Clarita that whole in-contract stretch is usually about 30–45 days.

The part nobody tells buyers

The strongest buyers don't win because they overpay. They win because they're ready — money lined up, real pre-approval in hand, clear on the neighborhood, and decisive when the right home shows up. That readiness is the entire point of this site. Get it handled before you're standing in someone's living room on a Saturday.
